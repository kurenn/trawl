Keep a copy of any shared folder.

Paste a Google Drive or pCloud folder link. Trawl copies it to your computer and keeps it up to date. It only ever adds files — it never deletes anything.

Download for macOS
brew install --cask kurenn/tap/trawl

macOS, Windows and Linux · Free and open source · No accounts, no tracking

A copy that looks after itself.

Point Trawl at a shared folder once. From then on it checks for new files and brings them home, without touching anything already there.

Paste a link

A Google Drive folder link or a pCloud public link is all it needs. Look inside and pick what to copy before anything moves.

See it copy

How far along it is, how much is left and how long it will take — for every folder.

Stays up to date

Trawl checks for new files on a schedule you choose. Copies are incremental, so a check with nothing new is quick.

Never deletes

Copies only add files. Nothing at your end is ever removed, and your own edits are kept safe instead of being overwritten.

Browse what you have

Look through any folder's files from inside Trawl, open a file, or jump to the folder in your file manager.

Problems come with a fix

A drive that isn't connected or a folder that loops back on itself shows what happened and a one-click way to carry on.

Nothing else to install

The copying engine, rclone, ships inside the app. Install with Homebrew or a one-line script.

Yours, privately

No accounts and no tracking. Your Google sign-in stays on your computer.

From link to library in three steps.

Trawl only ever pulls: no uploading, no two-way sync, and nothing deleted at your end.

01

Paste a folder link

Trawl shows what's inside. Choose everything or one subfolder, and rename where it lands if you like. Google Drive asks you to sign in once; pCloud links need nothing.

02

Let it copy

Watch the progress or close the window — Trawl keeps going in the menu bar. Stopping keeps everything copied so far.

03

Open what arrived

Every folder has its own page: browse its files, open them, and see where it lives on your computer.
